Nine engagements, one recurring question: does the record match the physical reality, and what breaks first when it doesn't? These three are the most directly relevant; the diagnosis never changed across any of them.

Case Study · Process Improvement

Restoring Control to a Failing Account

I took over an unsatisfactory supply chain account and designed the first-line controls that restored it to full compliance. Three control artefacts were adopted, creating a process that did not depend on one individual.

Result: Full compliance restored; three control artefacts adopted; process no longer dependent on one individual.

Civilian relevance: control design, audit remediation and process improvement that survives a change of owner.

Case Study · Financial Control

Identifying £2.4M in Misallocated Funds

I identified £2.4M in misallocated expenditure through systematic analysis of spending patterns and led the root-cause analysis that supported its correction. The findings informed a proposed enterprise-wide policy instruction to prevent recurrence.

Result: £2.4M in misallocated expenditure identified; policy instruction proposed to prevent recurrence.

Civilian relevance: root-cause analysis, financial control investigation and evidence-based policy recommendations.

Case Study · Digital Transformation

Building Fleet-Wide Inventory Visibility

I took over a predecessor's incomplete build and reconfigured the spreadsheet feeding it, creating a single source of truth for STOROB (cross-platform stock reallocation) tracking across upkeep units. It was rolled out to an adjacent unit, and inventory managers elsewhere in the fleet have since adopted it.

Result: Single source of truth for STOROB tracking; adopted by an adjacent unit and by inventory managers elsewhere in the fleet.

Civilian relevance: requirements ownership, data redesign, and completing a stalled build rather than starting one from scratch.
