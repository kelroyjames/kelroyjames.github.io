◆ Career Chief Naval Logistics Officer's Award

Dangerous Goods Training and a Foundation Degree, at the Same Time

In 2018, as the sole logistics point of contact for Fleet Diving Squadron HQ and Fleet Diving Group, I was delivering dangerous goods awareness training to EOD personnel and finishing a Foundation Degree at the same time. The Chief Naval Logistics Officer's Award, signed by Rear Admiral C R Gardner CBE, cites both.

Kelroy James September 2026
📖 3 min read

The Chief Naval Logistics Officer's Award for the 2017/18 training year was presented for the Supply Chain Branch, through the Defence Maritime Logistics School, for the rating judged to have made the most significant contribution to delivering or promoting high quality training and coaching that year. I received it while covering two units at once, and while finishing the Foundation Degree I have written about elsewhere on this site.

One Person, Two Units

By 2018 I was the sole logistics and supply chain point of contact for two units at once, Fleet Diving Squadron HQ and Fleet Diving Group. The citation describes a dedicated service delivering support to a wide range of customers and consistently achieving results, with the confidence of the chain of command to take on additional responsibility without a senior rate above me to escalate to. That is the same structural gap the Fleet Diving Squadron work-based project was built to close: a unit with more logistics demand than logistics establishment.

Explosives, EOD Training, and Operation Tapestry

Part of that additional responsibility was being trusted to handle and administer dangerous goods, including explosives, and to liaise directly with external organisations where needed. The citation records that I went on to deliver dangerous goods awareness training to Explosive Ordnance Disposal personnel based in the Portsmouth area, and that I maintained Operation Tapestry, the Royal Navy's dangerous goods and explosives safety programme, throughout. Alongside that, I wrote the Standard Operating Procedures the unit did not already have: for the demand and return of dive cylinders, for moving permanent and limited items back through the reverse supply chain, and for categorising stores correctly in the first place.

Mentoring Ahead of the Qualifying Course

The citation also credits mentoring rather than just delivery. I was coaching peers and subordinates on MJDI and supply chain knowledge before they sat their Qualifying Course at the RN Supply Chain school, and the HMS Excellent Stores Manager routinely commended the proactive support this gave the wider department. None of that shows up as a metric. It shows up, eventually, as other people passing their own courses.

The Same Year as the Degree

The citation itself closes by naming the Foundation Degree in Logistics Management I had just completed with the University of Lincoln, and a Level 5 Award in Management and Leadership presented around the same time. Reading it back now, it is a citation that credits study and delivery in the same sentence, which is the same pattern the rest of my reporting history shows: whatever I was studying at the time is usually the thing the next report describes me actually doing.
