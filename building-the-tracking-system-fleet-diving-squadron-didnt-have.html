Building the Tracking System Fleet Diving Squadron Didn't Have

In 2017, as the Logistics Specialist at Fleet Diving Squadron, I completed a Foundation Degree in Logistics Management with the University of Lincoln. The work-based project was a PRINCE2 proposal to install a consignment-tracking system the unit didn't have, tested live on a NATO exercise in Latvia within the year.

Kelroy James September 2026
📖 4 min read

The BSc I wrote about recently was not the only credential missing from the published account of my career. Before Aston, there was a Foundation Degree in Logistics Management with the University of Lincoln, completed in 2018 through work-based learning while I was serving as the Logistics Specialist at Fleet Diving Squadron. Its centrepiece was a capstone module called Work Based Logistics Project, which required a real organisational problem, managed as a formal project rather than written up as a case study.

A Foundation Degree Built Around the Unit

I joined Fleet Diving Squadron in December 2016, responsible for advising Command on all logistics matters. The Squadron is organised across Fleet Diving Units 1 to 3, Southern Diving Unit 2 and an Operational Support Unit, held at high readiness to counter mine and explosive threats worldwide. The capstone module asked students to identify a genuine problem inside their own workplace, then run it through a full project lifecycle: initiation, risk management, stakeholder analysis, phased delivery and a final progress evaluation, marked against Masters-adjacent academic criteria despite sitting within a Foundation Degree.

The Problem: No System to Track What Left the Building

Fleet Diving Squadron had a legal obligation under HMRC's Project Waterguard to consign every piece of equipment leaving or re-entering the UK for exercises and deployments. The unit had no consignment-tracking hardware or software of its own to do this, relying on manual procedures and on external units to produce the paperwork, with a single supply chain individual coordinating the whole process. A failure to comply carried financial liability. A failure to produce documentation on time carried the risk of a cancelled exercise.

My recommendation was to install VITALS, the Ministry of Defence's Visibility In Tracking Assets Logistics System, giving the Squadron its own consignment-tracking capability rather than depending on units outside the building.

Running It as PRINCE2, Not as an Essay

The project was structured using PRINCE2, the same methodology I would later apply again in defence and industry contexts. A Project Initiation Document set out scope, deliverables and interfaces with the Defence Logistics Framework. SWOT and PESTLE analyses grounded the case in the Squadron's actual constraints: a strong technical workforce and physical infrastructure set against a shortage of logistics personnel, IT hardware and a formal movements structure. A risk register tracked technical, planning and relationship risks through to closure, with contingency funding set aside for delays.

Delivery ran across four phases: installing the VITALS hardware and software in August, training staff through September and into October, fitting the supporting printers and calibrated scales, and then testing the whole system live on Exercise Open Spirit in Latvia that October. The same capability went on to support Exercise Sea Breeze in Ukraine, Exercise Northern Challenge and Exercise Joint Warrior, the recurring exercise cycle the Squadron depends on to stay deployable.

What Carried Forward

The Royal Navy issued a call across the Fleet in spring 2026 for volunteers to build dedicated "movements" expertise, covering dangerous-goods competency and consignment-tracking documentation of exactly the kind this project installed at Fleet Diving Squadron in 2017. The unit-level gap I closed then is still, at Fleet level, a gap the Navy is actively working to close now. The PRINCE2 discipline behind it, structured risk management, phased delivery, and compliance built into the process rather than bolted on afterward, is the same discipline running through everything else I have written on this site since.
