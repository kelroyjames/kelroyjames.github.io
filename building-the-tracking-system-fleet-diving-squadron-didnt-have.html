Building the Tracking System a High-Readiness Unit Didn't Have

In 2017, as the Logistics Specialist at a high-readiness Royal Navy unit, I completed a Foundation Degree in Logistics Management with the University of Lincoln. The work-based project was a PRINCE2 proposal to install a consignment-tracking system the unit didn't have, tested live on a NATO exercise in Latvia within the year.

Kelroy James September 2026
📖 4 min read

The BSc I wrote about recently was not the only credential missing from the published account of my career. Before Aston, there was a Foundation Degree in Logistics Management with the University of Lincoln, completed in 2018 through work-based learning while I was serving as the Logistics Specialist at a high-readiness Royal Navy unit. Its centrepiece was a capstone module called Work Based Logistics Project, which required a real organisational problem, managed as a formal project rather than written up as a case study. Mine was a problem the unit was living with daily, not one selected to make a tidy assignment.

A Foundation Degree Built Around the Unit

I joined the unit in December 2016, responsible for advising Command on all logistics matters. The unit is organised across several subordinate elements, held at high readiness to counter mine and explosive threats worldwide. The capstone module asked students to identify a genuine problem inside their own workplace, then run it through a full project lifecycle: initiation, risk management, stakeholder analysis, phased delivery and a final progress evaluation, marked against Masters-adjacent academic criteria despite sitting within a Foundation Degree. A high-readiness unit with a small logistics footprint was not short of genuine problems to choose from.

What Left the Building

The unit had a legal obligation under HMRC's Project Waterguard to consign every piece of equipment leaving or re-entering the UK for exercises and deployments. The unit had no consignment-tracking hardware or software of its own to do this, relying on manual procedures and on external units to produce the paperwork, with a single supply chain individual coordinating the whole process. A failure to comply carried financial liability. A failure to produce documentation on time carried the risk of a cancelled exercise.

My recommendation was to install VITALS, the Ministry of Defence's Visibility In Tracking Assets Logistics System, giving the unit its own consignment-tracking capability rather than depending on units outside the building. That meant the unit would no longer be waiting on someone else's paperwork to move its own equipment, and it would own the compliance record rather than borrowing it. VITALS was already a Ministry of Defence system, which meant no new procurement case was needed, only the case for installing it here. That case still had to be made against the unit's actual constraints rather than assumed.

Structured as a PRINCE2 Project

The project was structured using PRINCE2, the same methodology I would later apply again in defence and industry contexts. A Project Initiation Document set out scope, deliverables and interfaces with the Defence Logistics Framework. SWOT and PESTLE analyses grounded the case in the unit's actual constraints: a strong technical workforce and physical infrastructure set against a shortage of logistics personnel, IT hardware and a formal movements structure. A risk register tracked technical, planning and relationship risks through to closure, with contingency funding set aside for delays.

Delivery ran across four phases: installing the VITALS hardware and software in August, training staff through September and into October, fitting the supporting printers and calibrated scales and then testing the whole system live on Exercise Open Spirit in Latvia that October. The same capability went on to support Exercise Sea Breeze in Ukraine, Exercise Northern Challenge and Exercise Joint Warrior, the recurring exercise cycle the unit depends on to stay deployable. A system that only worked once, on the exercise it was built for, would have been a proof of concept rather than a genuine capability. Carrying it across three further exercises is what turned it into one.

4 phases · 4 exercises · 1 legal obligation

A PRINCE2-managed system delivery against a live HMRC consignment-tracking requirement, tested on Exercise Open Spirit within the year and carried forward across three further multinational exercises

What Carried Forward

The Royal Navy issued a call across the Fleet in spring 2026 for volunteers to build dedicated "movements" expertise, covering dangerous-goods competency and consignment-tracking documentation of exactly the kind this project installed at unit level in 2017. The unit-level fix I built then anticipated a need the Navy has since begun addressing fleet-wide. Civilian relevance: this is PRINCE2 project delivery run against a live regulatory compliance obligation, structured risk management, phased delivery and evidence built into the process rather than bolted on afterward. That combination is the same one a compliance-systems, third-party risk-tooling or trade-documentation role in industry is built around.
