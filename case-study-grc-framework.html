Modernising Assurance Across Inspections

Re-engineering deck-plate assurance across three Fleet Logistics Inspections through integrated ISO governance, Lean Six Sigma, and Three Lines Model.

Role GRC Framework Lead
Timeline Mar 2025 – Jul 2026 (16 months)
Organisation Royal Navy
Scope Enterprise-level transformation

TL;DR

Situation

Three Fleet Logistics Inspection failures in fifteen months exposed person-dependent controls with no systematic governance framework behind them.

Action

Designed control mechanisms from first principles using ISO 27001, 42001, and 27701, Lean Six Sigma, and the Three Lines Model, then embedded the change through policy and academic validation.

Result

Full compliance sustained after handover, with the controls still holding under the next account holder.

The Challenge

Operational gaps across three Fleet Logistics Inspections (FLIs) in 15 months revealed systemic deficiencies in deck-plate assurance. The existing approach relied on position power rather than systematic controls, creating compliance gaps that couldn't be sustained through personnel changes.

The Core Problem

Traditional naval assurance relied on the person, not the process. When that person left, the controls failed. The challenge was to design transferable governance that worked independently of who was in the role.

Specific Challenges:

  • Inspection failure pattern: Three FLIs in 15 months exposed recurring control weaknesses
  • Compliance gaps: Significant deficiencies inherited from previous management
  • Person-dependent controls: Assurance relied on individual expertise, not documented processes
  • No governance framework: Royal Navy BRd 93 standards existed but lacked systematic implementation methodology
  • Cultural resistance: Junior ratings not positioned as first-line risk owners in Three Lines Model

Strategic Context

Rather than treating this as a compliance remediation exercise, I recognised it as an opportunity to apply commercial governance frameworks to military logistics. The question was: could ISO governance standards, Lean Six Sigma, and Three Lines Model close the gap between policy and practice at the operational level?

Approach

Greenfield Continuous Improvement Strategy

Adopted a greenfield approach rather than attempting to fix inherited broken processes. This meant designing new control mechanisms from first principles, grounded in commercial governance standards and academic change management theory.

Power Model Selection: Expert & Referent Over Position

Applied French & Raven's power taxonomy strategically. Rather than relying on rank (position power), built influence through demonstrated expertise (expert power) and cultural engagement (referent power). This was critical for embedding change that would survive leadership turnover.

Integrated Governance Framework

Combined four complementary standards to create a comprehensive assurance system:

  • ISO 42001 (AI Management): Governance for analytics dashboard and data-driven decision-making
  • ISO 27001 (Information Security): Controls for financial data integrity and access management
  • ISO 27701 (Privacy): Personnel data protection in performance tracking systems
  • Lean Six Sigma Green Belt: Process efficiency, waste elimination, continuous improvement discipline

Mapped these frameworks against Royal Navy BRd 93 logistics standards to demonstrate that commercial governance and military compliance were describing the same controls in different vocabularies.

Three Lines Model

Re-positioned deck-plate roles within the 3LoD model:

  • 1st Line (Risk Ownership): Junior ratings as first-line risk owners, not just task executors
  • 2nd Line (Oversight): NCO/SNCO layer providing policy guidance and exception management
  • 3rd Line (Assurance): Fleet Logistics Inspection as independent verification mechanism

Change Management Theory

Grounded the transformation in academic organisational change frameworks to ensure sustainability beyond my tenure. This became the basis for MSc-level research validating the approach.

Implementation: Control Mechanisms

New Control Mechanisms Established

1. Standardised Loss Validation Routines

Designed systematic protocols for investigating and documenting inventory discrepancies. Moved from ad-hoc "find the lost item" efforts to structured root-cause analysis using 5 Whys and fishbone diagrams.

2. Voucher Assurance Controls

Created financial transaction verification processes that caught the £2.4M misallocation. Established segregation of duties, approval hierarchies, and exception reporting thresholds.

3. Enterprise Analytics Dashboard

Co-designed custom dashboard providing real-time visibility into:

  • Stock variance trends by category and location
  • Financial liability exposure by account line
  • Compliance status against BRd 93 requirements
  • Control effectiveness metrics (detection rate, closure time, recurrence)

The £2.4M Discovery

The analytics dashboard surfaced spending patterns that didn't align with operational activity. Systematic voucher analysis revealed £2.4M in misallocated expenditure across multiple account lines. This was systemic control failure, not fraud: transactions were recorded against incorrect budget codes, creating both financial liability and inventory discrepancies.

Recovery required coordinating with finance teams, validating actual usage, correcting ledger entries, and establishing preventive controls to stop recurrence.

Infrastructure Resilience Business Case

Drafted business case for infrastructure upgrades to support the new control environment. Demonstrated ROI through risk reduction, efficiency gains, and inspection readiness. Business case approved and funded.

Inspection Recovery

Outcome: Full compliance rating on subsequent Fleet Logistics Inspection. Inspection report noted the transformation from unsatisfactory to compliant and highlighted control mechanisms as transferable best practice.

Institutionalisation: Scaling Beyond the Individual

The critical test of any assurance framework is whether it survives the departure of the person who designed it. To institutionalise this transformation, I operated on three parallel tracks: policy adoption, academic validation, and divisional awareness.

1. Policy Adoption: Junior Ratings Empowerment Day

Designed and piloted "JR Empowerment Day" to reframe junior ratings, accountable for their own controls rather than only following senior direction, as 1st Line Risk Owners under the Three Lines Model.

Programme Structure:

  • Explained 3LoD architecture in operational context (not corporate theory)
  • Mapped daily tasks to control objectives (showed them they were already running controls)
  • Taught exception escalation protocols (when to escalate vs. resolve locally)
  • Demonstrated how their observations fed into risk management decisions

Cultural Shift: From "I'm told what to do" to "I own this process and escalate when control boundaries are breached."

Outcome: Initiative formally adopted as Departmental Policy. Now runs as standing programme, not one-off training event. This is the institutional lock-in: the cultural change is now policy-mandated and resourced.

2. Academic Validation: MSc Research

Validated the transformation academically through two MSc dissertations, ensuring the approach was grounded in peer-reviewed theory and reproducible by others:

MSc Organisational Leadership & Change:

  • Research title: "Reframing Junior Ratings as First-Line Risk Owners: A Three Lines of Defence Cultural Change Model"
  • Validated JR Empowerment Day using Kotter's 8-step change model and French & Raven's power taxonomy
  • Demonstrated that expert and referent power were more effective than position power for embedding cultural change
  • Produced transferable change management framework for similar military-to-civilian governance transitions

MSc Decision Support:

  • Research focus: "Modularisation of Royal Navy Training Pipelines: Optimising Force Generation Through Decision Analytics"
  • Analysed how training pipeline design impacts operational readiness and skills availability
  • Developed decision support framework for balancing throughput, quality, and adaptability
  • Findings applicable to corporate capability development and workforce planning

3. Divisional Awareness: GRC Skills Mapping

Presented integrated GRC/Lean Six Sigma skills-mapping to Engineering Support Group leadership. Demonstrated how naval logistics checks map directly to corporate GRC roles (compliance analyst, internal auditor, risk manager, quality assurance specialist).

Strategic Impact: This addresses the military-to-civilian transition gap. Service leavers can now articulate their experience in corporate governance language, and civilian employers can recognise the commercial value of military logistics discipline.

Outcomes

  • £2.4M misallocated expenditure identified and corrected: Voucher assurance controls caught systemic budget code errors; full recovery executed through ledger corrections and preventive controls
  • Unsatisfactory → Fully Compliant inspection rating: Fleet Logistics Inspection outcome transformed through systematic control design, not person-dependent effort
  • 3 Fleet Logistics Inspections navigated in 15 months: High operational tempo provided multiple validation cycles for the new control framework
  • Enterprise analytics dashboard operational: Real-time visibility into compliance status, financial exposure, and control effectiveness; scoped for wider fleet adoption
  • Departmental policy adoption (JR Empowerment Day): Cultural change initiative embedded as standing programme, ensuring sustainability beyond individual tenure
  • Two MSc dissertations: Academic validation of the transformation methodology, producing transferable frameworks
  • Infrastructure resilience business case approved: Investment secured for system upgrades supporting the new control environment
  • Divisional awareness established: Engineering Support Group leadership now recognises GRC/LSS skills as transferable to civilian roles

The Transferability Test

The true measure of success: when I departed the role, the controls continued to function. The subsequent FLI (conducted after my departure) maintained the Fully Compliant rating. This confirms the framework was process-driven, not person-dependent.

The Translation Challenge This Project Solves

Military logistics professionals often struggle to translate their experience into civilian governance language. This project demonstrates that the skills are identical. Only the vocabulary differs:

  • "Fleet Logistics Inspection" = Internal audit / regulatory inspection
  • "BRd 93 compliance" = ISO 9001 / sector-specific regulatory compliance
  • "Voucher assurance" = Financial controls / SOX compliance
  • "Stock variance protocol" = Inventory reconciliation / shrinkage management
  • "Junior ratings as 1st Line Risk Owners" = Embedded control ownership / risk-aware culture

By mapping military assurance to ISO frameworks and the Three Lines Model, I've created a bridge that allows civilian employers to see the commercial value of Defence logistics expertise.
