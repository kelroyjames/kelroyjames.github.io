Verified, not self-reported

Skills translation for service leavers usually runs on trust — take my word that the job maps to what I say it maps to. This runs on the Royal Navy's own published role descriptions instead, rank by rank, trade by trade, checked against public civilian control frameworks.

My earlier table, Same Control, Two Vocabularies, translated my own eighteen years in one trade. Useful for me, but it asks you to trust a stranger's self-report. This page tries to remove that step: instead of describing my own job, it pairs the Royal Navy's own published recruitment role descriptions — the same ones a school-leaver reads before applying — with the nearest civilian control language, rank by rank.

It starts with Logistics because that is the trade I can check against my own experience. Engineering, Warfare and Healthcare & Medical follow, built the same way but without a serving logistician's gut-check behind them — treat those three as a first pass, and see the caution below.

This also sits inside a bigger shift already under way inside Defence: the Pan-Defence Skills Framework, referenced in JSP 822, is building a single skills vocabulary across the services. That framework's detail sits inside internal MOD systems, not a public dataset, so nothing here draws on it beyond its published existence. Everything below comes from pages anyone can read at royalnavy.mod.uk.

What this table is not

The Logistics row is checked against my own service. Engineering, Warfare and Healthcare & Medical are built from the same public recruitment pages, but I have not done those jobs, and recruitment copy is written to attract applicants, not to document duties — it is thinner and more polished than an internal job specification. Treat those three trades as a starting point to correct, not a finished mapping.

It is not a compliance mapping. No organisation should treat a row here as evidence that a requirement has been met — the frameworks named are pointers to where the equivalent control is written down, not claims of conformity.

And a caution I'd apply to my own argument: naming a control is not evidence that it worked. The value of this page is in helping someone ask better questions about their own experience, not in letting them assert more than they've earned.

Help correct or extend it

If you served in Engineering, Warfare or Healthcare & Medical and the middle column doesn't match what the job actually involved, or if you want your own trade added, email me your trade and rank — this page gets more accurate every time someone who's held the job corrects it, and that's the only way the mapping earns the word "verified" beyond Logistics.
