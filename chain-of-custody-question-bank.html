One of the twenty-five

Q03 · Knowing who you buy from

When a supplier changes factory, sub-supplier or ownership, how do you find out, and how quickly?

Evidence that answers it

  • A contractual duty on the supplier to notify changes
  • A change log comparing the date notified with the date the change took effect
  • The re-assessments each change triggered

Warning sign

Changes are discovered from shipping documents after the goods have moved.

Anchored in · ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.22 · ISO 9001:2015 clause 8.4

Why these questions

Most supply chain controls fail quietly, at the point where a record stops matching the goods, the supplier or the team that is meant to act on it. The questions are written to find those points before a regulator, a customer or an auditor does, and a strong answer to any of them is a document, a log or a record, whilst a weak answer is a description of what is supposed to happen.

I wrote them as a lead auditor for ISO/IEC 27001, ISO/IEC 42001 and ISO/IEC 27701, and as someone who has spent a career running first-line supply chain controls.
