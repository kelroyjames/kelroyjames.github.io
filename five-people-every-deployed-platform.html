◆ Career Herbert Lott Efficiency Award

Five People, Every Deployed Platform

In 2019, the five members of the Fleet Stores Coordination Cell at Northwood, including me, received the Herbert Lott Efficiency Award as a team. What we did at once for the whole deployed Royal Navy, and why the citation credits the cell rather than any one of us.

Kelroy James September 2026
📖 3 min read

The Herbert Lott Efficiency Award sits on my CV as a single line: 2019, Fleet Stores Coordination Cell. That line undersells it in one direction and oversells it in another. It undersells the scale of what the cell was doing. It oversells what any one of us did alone, because the award went to five of us together, and the citation says so explicitly.

Five People, Not a Single Unit's Worth of Work

The Fleet Stores Coordination Cell was part of an NCHQ team embedded in the Maritime Operations Centre at Northwood. What made the cell unusual was scope. Most logistics roles at that rank support one ship or one unit. This one supported every Royal Navy and Royal Marines platform on operations anywhere in the world, providing 24/7 cover and moving operational-defect material through military and commercial supply chains wherever a deployed platform needed it. The citation calls the level of autonomy and decision-making involved "rarely bestowed on a Leading Hand." That description applied equally to all five of us.

Three Operations, at the Same Time

The award citation names three concurrent commitments rather than a single achievement. The Royal Navy was re-establishing itself as a carrier navy through WESTLANT 18, the deployment marking the first-of-class F-35 Joint Strike Fighter flying trials, and the cell worked directly with the afloat Carrier Strike Group staff, the Forward Logistics Site team, and US customs to keep it moving. At the same time, the cell was sustaining a second Forward Logistics Site in the Middle East, supporting the Amphibious Task Group and the standing UKMCC framework so the amphibious contribution to SAIF SAREEA 3 could go ahead. On top of both, Exercise Trident Juncture landed at the tail end of 2018, in the High North, at considerable distance from any support hub. None of these were sequential. They ran together, watchkept around the clock by five people.

What the Team Gave Up to Do It

The citation is specific about the personal cost as well as the operational output. It credits the flexibility the five of us, and our families, showed in absorbing a disrupted watch bill so that individual career development still happened during that period: an Admiralty Interview Board, professional and leadership courses, and a period of paternity leave for one of the team, all fitted around coverage that could not lapse. The citation's own summary is blunt about the stakes: in that environment, a delay of a few minutes could become a delay of hours or days in mission-essential support, with a direct effect on operational capability. The award recognises that the cell held that line anyway, for the length of the assignment, as a team.

Why the Framing Matters

I could describe this award using only my own actions and it would still be true in outline. It would also misrepresent what actually happened. Five Leading Hands held global, round-the-clock coverage across three concurrent operations, and the Royal Navy and Royal Marines Charity recognised all five. That's who earned it, and that's how I'm crediting it here.
