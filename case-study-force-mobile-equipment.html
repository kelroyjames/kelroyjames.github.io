Closing Asset-Accountability Gaps

Bringing dispersed ground support equipment under one accountable stock holding site, and closing fourteen major loss investigations along the way.

Role Supply Chain & Global Movements (Petty Officer)
Timeline Apr 2022 – May 2024
Organisation Wildcat Maritime Force, Royal Navy
Outcome Tier 3 Recommendation Delivered

TL;DR

Situation

Fourteen major loss investigations open at once, with dispersed equipment holdings and no single point of accountability.

Action

Consolidated all Force Mobile Equipment into one accountable stock holding site and worked each open investigation to recovery rather than write-off.

Result

All fourteen investigations closed, and the site's scope later expanded fleet-wide.

Civilian relevance

Asset accountability, loss investigation and audit liaison.

The Challenge

Force Mobile Equipment is the ground support equipment that keeps a helicopter force serviceable: generators, tow tractors, maintenance stands and the rest of the kit engineers need to actually work on the aircraft. Rather than being tracked through a single accountable point, it was dispersed across squadron holdings. When equipment moved between users without a clean paper trail, loss investigations followed, and by the time I took on the stores office at Wildcat Maritime Force Headquarters there was a backlog of fourteen major loss cases open at once.

The Core Issue

A loss investigation only shows that an item is missing. It does not show why the system let it go missing, or how many more are about to. Fourteen open cases at once were not fourteen separate problems: they were one control gap, counted fourteen times.

Context & Stakes

Force Mobile Equipment is not incidental kit. When holding is dispersed and loosely tracked:

  • Serviceability is exposed – Equipment recorded as held may not actually be where it's needed when a squadron deploys
  • Investigations consume disproportionate time – Every loss triggers an investigation, and a standing backlog draws effort away from the work that would prevent the next one
  • Audit findings recur – Without one point of accountability, the same class of loss keeps reappearing at inspection
  • Command loses confidence in the account – A stores account with a persistent loss backlog gets watched more closely, which costs everyone's time, not just the account holder's

The fourteen open cases were the visible symptom. The underlying issue was that nobody held clean, single-point accountability for where Force Mobile Equipment actually was.

Approach

Centralising Control

Following a formal audit recommendation, I project-managed the transfer of all Force Mobile Equipment from its dispersed holding arrangement into a single, accountable stock holding site under direct stores control. That meant tracking down equipment spread across multiple holders, reconciling what each one actually had against what the ledger said they should have and re-establishing custody under one point of accountability rather than several.

The project ran over its original timescale. Consolidating equipment fleet-wide while keeping daily stores output running, with a limited workforce, was a heavier undertaking than the schedule allowed for. I judged that doing it properly mattered more than hitting the original date, and completed it to a standard the account could actually sustain afterwards rather than one that would simply reopen the same problem six months later.

Redesigning the Supply Chain Section Around It

Centralising the equipment also meant rethinking how the supply chain section itself was laid out, drawing on lessons from previous units, so that it could support both the home base and deployed detachments without stretching a limited workforce past what it could sustain.

Closing the Investigations

Consolidation dealt with where the equipment would live going forward. It did nothing for the fourteen cases already open. Closing those meant working each one individually: tracing the equipment's paper trail, cross-referencing handover records and in most cases physically locating items that had been recorded as lost rather than accepting the loss and writing them off.

The majority of the fourteen were closed by recovery rather than write-off. That distinction mattered to Command and to the audit function reviewing the account: a recovered item closes cleanly, while a written-off one leaves a value gap that consolidation alone doesn't fix. I also worked directly with the officers running the remaining investigations to keep them moving inside the timescales the process requires, rather than let them drift.

Outcomes

  • Force Mobile Equipment Centralised – All FME brought under a single accountable stock holding site, following a Tier 3 audit recommendation
  • 14 Major Loss Investigations Closed – The great majority resolved by recovering the missing equipment rather than writing it off
  • Supply Chain Section Restructured – Layout redesigned to support both the home base and deployed detachments without over-extending a limited workforce
  • Sustained and Expanded – The consolidated site's scope was expanded the following reporting year to bring dispersed squadron holdings under the same accountable control
  • Positively Received by Command and Audit – The closure of the loss backlog was recognised at both unit and Tier 3 level

Consolidation Is a Control Measure

Moving equipment into one site removes the condition that let losses happen in the first place: nobody could be held accountable for equipment nobody could definitively locate. Centralising custody makes the next loss investigation unnecessary, not just easier.

Lessons Learned

Treat the Backlog as a Single Problem

Fourteen open investigations look like fourteen separate failures. Treated individually, each one gets closed and the underlying condition remains. Treated as a single control gap, equipment without one accountable holding point, the fix addresses all fourteen at once and prevents the fifteenth.

Recovery Beats Write-Off

Writing off a lost item closes the paperwork. Finding it closes the actual problem. The extra effort of tracing rather than writing off is what audit and Command actually notice, because it demonstrates the account is under control rather than just administratively tidy.

Slipping the Date Is Sometimes the Right Call

A consolidation delivered on time but done badly just creates a new version of the same problem later. Delivering it slightly late, but to a standard the account can actually sustain, is the better trade, provided the reason for the slip is the scale of the work, not a lack of grip on it.

Design the Section Around the Workforce Actually Available

A supply chain section covering both a home base and deployed detachments cannot be designed as if it had unlimited people. Restructuring around the workforce actually available, rather than the workforce the task would ideally need, is what makes a control sustainable rather than dependent on heroics.

Civilian Relevance

Asset accountability, loss investigation and audit liaison: turning a backlog of individual failures into a single control fix is exactly the diagnostic work an asset-management, internal audit or stock-loss function is asked to do.
